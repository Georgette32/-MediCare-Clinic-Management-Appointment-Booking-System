# MediCare – Clinic Management System
## Business Requirements & Business Logic – Updated Version

---

# 1. Project Overview

MediCare is a Clinic Management System designed to organize and optimize the daily operations of medical clinics by connecting patients, doctors, receptionists, and administrators through one centralized platform.

The system manages the complete patient journey, starting from discovering doctors and booking appointments, through clinic check-in and queue management, until medical consultation and record management.

MediCare is not just an appointment booking application. It is an operational management system focused on optimizing clinic capacity, reducing patient waiting time, and improving the efficiency of medical services.

## 2. Primary Business Goal

The primary objective of MediCare is:

**Minimize patient waiting time inside the clinic while maximizing the number of patients served without compromising service quality or disrupting scheduled appointments.**

The system aims to achieve this through:

- Intelligent appointment scheduling.
- Efficient patient queue management.
- Fair handling of booked and walk-in patients.
- Monitoring doctor service times.
- Applying Queueing Theory to analyze clinic operations.
- Providing real-time operational insights through the dashboard.
- Optimizing the utilization of doctors and available working hours.

### Business Success Indicators

The system should help the clinic monitor and improve:

1. Average patient waiting time.
2. Average total time spent inside the clinic.
3. Number of patients served per doctor.
4. Number of patients waiting at any given time.
5. Doctor utilization.
6. Queue length throughout working hours.
7. The difference between scheduled and actual patient arrival times.

# 3. Business Problems

Traditional clinic management often suffers from:

1. Unorganized appointment scheduling.
2. Long patient waiting times.
3. Inefficient utilization of doctors' working hours.
4. Conflicts between scheduled appointments.
5. Difficulty handling late patients.
6. Unstructured walk-in patient management.
7. Lack of visibility into actual queue conditions.
8. Difficulty estimating the number of patients a doctor can serve.
9. Lack of analytical information for operational decisions.
10. Fragmented patient medical records.

MediCare addresses these problems by combining appointment management, queue management, and operational analytics.

# 7. Patient Queue Management

Queue management is one of the most important business modules in MediCare.

The system handles two different patient categories:

1. Patients with scheduled appointments.
2. Walk-in patients without scheduled appointments.

These categories have different priority and ordering rules.

## 7.1 Scheduled Patients Queue

Patients who have confirmed appointments enter the consultation process according to their scheduled appointment order.

**Business Rules:**
- Appointment priority is determined by the booking schedule.
- Patients are expected to arrive before or at their scheduled time.
- Reception records the actual arrival time.
- A late patient is subject to the Grace Period policy.
- A walk-in patient cannot automatically override the scheduled appointment order.

## 7.2 Walk-in Patients Queue

Walk-in patients are patients who arrive without a confirmed appointment and request medical consultation.

**Queue Ordering: FIFO – First In, First Out**

Walk-in patients are ordered according to their actual arrival time at the clinic.

The earliest registered walk-in patient receives the highest priority within the walk-in queue.

**Important distinction:**

FIFO applies specifically to walk-in patients. Scheduled patients are served according to their appointment order, not their physical arrival order.

## 7.3 Grace Period Management

Grace Period is a configurable period that determines how long a scheduled patient can arrive late while retaining the opportunity to receive their scheduled appointment.

The Grace Period is Custom and is controlled according to clinic policy.

### Late Patient Replacement Workflow

This is the approved workflow for handling a scheduled patient who has not arrived when their appointment time begins.

**Step 1: Scheduled Appointment Time**

- The patient's scheduled appointment time arrives.
- The patient has not checked in.
- The system identifies the patient as late.
- The Grace Period countdown starts.

**Step 2: Walk-in Replacement**

- The clinic can allow a waiting walk-in patient to enter instead of leaving the doctor idle.
- The walk-in patient must follow the normal walk-in approval process.
- The doctor must approve this individual case.
- The walk-in patient enters the consultation.

**Step 3: Grace Period Runs in Parallel**

While the walk-in patient is receiving consultation, the original scheduled patient's Grace Period continues running.

There are two possible scenarios:

**Scenario A: Scheduled Patient Arrives Within Grace Period**

- The scheduled patient arrives before the Grace Period expires.
- The scheduled patient retains their appointment eligibility.
- They enter after the patient currently receiving consultation finishes.
- Their appointment priority is respected.
- They do not become a walk-in patient.

**Scenario B: Scheduled Patient Does Not Arrive Within Grace Period**

- The Grace Period expires.
- The patient is considered No-Show for their scheduled appointment.
- If the patient subsequently arrives, they are no longer treated as an active scheduled appointment.
- They are registered as a walk-in patient.
- All standard walk-in rules apply, including FIFO ordering and individual doctor approval.

### Important Rules

1. Grace Period starts when the scheduled appointment time arrives and the patient has not checked in.
2. The waiting walk-in replacement must have individual doctor approval.
3. The original scheduled patient's Grace Period continues independently while the replacement is receiving service.
4. If the original patient arrives within Grace Period, they retain their scheduled priority.
5. They enter after the patient currently receiving consultation finishes.
6. If the Grace Period expires without arrival, the appointment becomes No-Show.
7. Any subsequent arrival is handled as a new walk-in case.
8. No patient should be admitted through an unauthorized priority override.

## 7.4 Walk-in Admission Workflow

When a walk-in patient arrives:

1. Receptionist registers the patient.
2. Actual arrival time is recorded.
3. System checks doctor availability and scheduled appointments.
4. Patient is presented with available options.

### Option A: Alternative Appointment After the Last Scheduled Appointment

- The patient can be offered an alternative appointment after the last booked slot.
- If the appointment requires extending the doctor's working hours, doctor approval is mandatory.
- Working hours cannot be extended automatically.
- Once approved, the extended time is recorded.
- The patient receives the agreed appointment.

### Option B: Waiting Inside the Clinic

- Patient is registered in the walk-in queue.
- Patient is ordered according to FIFO.
- Patient waits until a consultation opportunity becomes available.
- An opportunity may occur when a scheduled patient fails to arrive or exceeds the Grace Period.
- Doctor approval is mandatory before admitting the walk-in patient.

## 7.5 Doctor Working Hours Extension

When additional patients need to be accommodated after the last scheduled appointment:

- Doctor approval is required.
- Extension start and end times must be recorded.
- The reason for extension must be documented.
- The extension must not happen automatically.

# 9. Queueing Theory & Intelligent Dashboard Analytics

## 9.1 Business Objective

One of MediCare's main objectives is to reduce patient waiting time by understanding and optimizing how patients move through the clinic.

Instead of depending only on fixed appointment times and manual queue management, the system uses operational data and Queueing Theory to estimate queue conditions and doctor capacity.

This enables the clinic to make better scheduling and operational decisions.

## 9.2 Data Collection

The system collects operational data for each doctor.

### Patient Arrival Data
- Scheduled appointment time.
- Actual patient arrival time.
- Check-in time.
- Actual consultation start time.

### Service Time Data
- Consultation start time.
- Consultation completion time.
- Actual service duration.
- Historical average service duration for each doctor.

### Queue Data
- Number of patients waiting.
- Number of patients receiving service.
- Number of patients registered in the system.
- Walk-in arrival patterns.
- Scheduled patient arrival patterns.

The data is used to calculate doctor-specific arrival and service characteristics.

## 9.3 Doctor-Specific Queueing Model

Each doctor has an independent service process.

The system calculates:

- Average patient arrival rate (λ).
- Average service rate (μ).
- Average service duration.
- Number of active service channels.
- Patient arrival distribution.
- Service time distribution.

Based on the collected data, the appropriate Queueing Theory model is selected.

The model must reflect the actual operating conditions rather than assuming all doctors have identical service behavior.

For example:
- M/M/1 may be suitable when arrivals and service times reasonably follow exponential distributions and one doctor serves one patient at a time.
- M/G/1 may be more suitable when arrival assumptions fit the model but service-time distribution is general.
- Other models may be required if actual clinic operations differ from these assumptions.

## 9.4 Queueing Metrics

The dashboard displays the following metrics for each doctor.

### Ls – Average Number of Patients in the System

The expected number of patients inside the doctor's service system.

Includes:
- Patients waiting.
- Patients currently receiving consultation.

### Lq – Average Number of Patients in the Queue

The expected number of patients waiting for consultation, excluding the patient currently being served.

### Ws – Average Time in the System

The expected total time a patient spends in the doctor's service system.

Includes:
- Waiting time.
- Actual consultation time.

### Wq – Average Waiting Time

The expected time a patient waits before receiving consultation.

These values are calculated according to the selected queueing model and its assumptions.

For a stable single-server system, Little's Law provides the relationships:

- Ls = λ × Ws
- Lq = λ × Wq

The system must also account for periods of high utilization where arrival rates approach or exceed service capacity.

## 9.5 Dashboard Visualization

The dashboard includes a dynamic graph showing the relationship between the number of patients and the clinic's working hours.

### Graph Requirements

- X-axis: Working hours throughout the day.
- Y-axis: Number of patients.
- Show changes in patient numbers throughout the working day.
- Display the expected queue size.
- Display the number of patients currently receiving service.
- Show the total expected number of patients in the system.
- Allow comparison between different doctors.
- Update results as new operational data becomes available.

The graph should help the clinic understand:
- Peak hours.
- Expected queue buildup.
- Periods with low patient volume.
- Doctor workload distribution.
- Opportunities to improve appointment scheduling.

## 9.6 Dashboard Refresh

The analytics are updated as operational data changes.

New arrival records and actual service durations are incorporated into the calculations.

The dashboard should distinguish between:
- Historical averages.
- Current observed queue conditions.
- Model-based predictions.

This distinction is important because theoretical averages are not necessarily identical to the actual number of patients currently waiting.

## 9.7 Business Value of Queueing Theory

The analytical module supports decisions such as:

1. Adjusting appointment intervals.
2. Identifying doctors with high waiting times.
3. Identifying peak clinic hours.
4. Improving doctor workload distribution.
5. Estimating the clinic's service capacity.
6. Reducing patient waiting time.
7. Improving patient throughput.
8. Evaluating the effect of walk-in admissions on scheduled appointments.

**Important:** The model is a decision-support mechanism. It does not replace the actual appointment priority rules or the doctor's approval authority.

# 10. Cancelled Appointment Recovery

When an appointment is cancelled:

1. Appointment status becomes Cancelled.
2. The corresponding time slot is released.
3. The slot becomes available according to scheduling rules.
4. Another patient may book the released slot.
5. The original cancellation remains in historical records.

The system must prevent double booking when the released slot is reused.
